# Fig. 2 | Global unit-capacity losses, climate–layout attribution and event composition

a–b, Global annual signed net generation loss per unit installed capacity, 2030–2059 (MWh MW⁻¹ yr⁻¹). Solid lines show equally weighted means of four climate models; translucent bands span model minima–maxima. Lines and bands connect successive years continuously, including capacity-snapshot boundaries. Dashed lines are ordinary least-squares fits to the 30 ensemble annual means, following the annual-trajectory reference. No annual point symbols or snapshot boundary lines are drawn. The fits describe the paired climate–deployment trajectories, not isolated climate responses or statistical significance.

c–d, Four-contribution attribution of the paired SSP585 minus SSP126 ten-year mean unit-capacity loss in 2030–2039, 2040–2049 and 2050–2059, with wind above solar. For each model and fixed deployment, R = E × CF_event × r_event, where E is annual capacity-weighted event-union hours, CF_event is normal event-period energy divided by capacity-weighted event hours, and r_event is signed net loss divided by normal event-period energy. The three climate contributions use a six-order Shapley decomposition of climate585 minus climate126 within deployment126 and deployment585, then average corresponding contributions across those deployments. The layout contribution averages deployment585 minus deployment126 at climate126 and climate585. The four contributions sum to the paired-pathway loss difference; interactions are allocated within these contributions. Calculations precede equal averaging over four models. Each decade shows climate exposure E, climate normal event-period generation CF, climate relative event loss r, layout S and net difference Δ. Contribution bars float from the preceding cumulative level; Δ starts at zero. Numbers are signed mean contributions in MWh MW⁻¹ yr⁻¹. Model ranges are retained in source tables. Exposure follows the loss-valid event union, and differs from Fig. 1 meteorological exposure. Layout captures changes in normalized capacity weights and locations, including their valid support; uniform capacity scaling cancels in R. Wind and solar have separately labelled y ranges.

e–f, Annualized event-labelled net losses per unit installed capacity, grouped by decade, with SSP126, SSP245 and SSP585 from left to right within each group. Each coloured segment is an equal-model mean of the ten-year event-specific net loss divided by the full valid installed capacity. Bar heights are physical loss quantities, not normalized to 100%. Each printed percentage is the segment mean divided by the sum of the displayed event means in its bar; labels are shown for shares of at least 5% when the segment is at least 7 points tall at the final figure size. All displayed event means are positive for these data; signed model values are preserved in the source tables and are averaged without clipping or taking absolute values. Event categories can overlap: the sum of event-labelled losses is not the event-union loss in a–b, and percentages are shares of this event-labelled sum, not exclusive attribution of the union. The event colours and order follow the event-composition reference. The event panels share their loss axis range.

Each climate SSP is paired with the corresponding deployment SSP. Capacity snapshots 2030, 2040 and 2050 apply to their respective complete ten-year windows. Global metrics are ratios of energy and capacity totals over the same valid stations. The accepted common support uses finite annual fields across climates, models, events and the ten years within each deployment snapshot; this does not establish identical three-hourly validity. The four models are CANESM5, MPI-ESM1-2-HR, MRI-ESM2-0 and BCC-CSM2-MR. Ranges are model spread, not confidence intervals. Wind energy retains its original scale. station_ssp585 originates from the SSP5-6.0 deployment file. Original Fig. 2 c–f are retained as Fig. S12 a–d.

Source tables: panel_ab.csv and panel_ab_display.csv (annual model values and ensemble statistics); annual_trends.csv (fits); global_window_models.csv and union_factors_models.csv (ten-year inputs); fig02_climate_layout_waterfall_2030s_2040s_2050s_{inputs,fixed_deployment,models,display}.csv (climate–layout contributions); event_composition_models.csv and event_composition_display.csv (event values, shares and label flags); capacity_coverage.csv (effective capacity). data_audit.json records completeness, normalization, input hashes and decomposition closure. metadata.json records products, code/source hashes and the visual review. The complete PNG and a+b, c+d, e+f groups are exported at 600 dpi.
